How to adopt a cat from a shelter

Shelters and humane societies have cats of every age and personality. Here's how to find the right one.

Kitten or adult?

Kittens are fun and a lot of work. Adult cats' personalities are already known, so it's easier to find a match. Senior cats are often the calmest companions.

Consider a bonded pair

Shelters often have pairs that must be adopted together, and some offer reduced fees for two. Two cats keep each other company while you're out.

Visit the cat room

Spend time with a few cats. Ask staff which ones are shy, playful or good with kids and other pets. Some shelters run cat lounges or cat cafés you can visit.

Apply and bring them home

Bring a photo ID and a carrier. The fee usually includes spay or neuter, vaccines and a microchip.

Settling in

Start your cat in one quiet room with food, water, a litter box and a hiding spot, then open up the house over days or weeks. See the adoption checklist.
