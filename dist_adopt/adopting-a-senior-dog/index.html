Adopting a senior dog

Older dogs often wait longest at shelters, yet many make calm, easy companions. Here's what to know.

Why adopt an older dog?

  • Their size, energy and personality are already known.
  • Many are house-trained and know basic manners.
  • They usually need less exercise than a young dog.
  • Some shelters reduce or waive fees for seniors.

What counts as senior?

It depends on size: large breeds are often considered seniors from about 6 or 7 years old, small dogs later. Ask the shelter how they estimate age.

Questions to ask

  • Any known health conditions, medications or dental issues?
  • Can I see recent vet records or bloodwork?
  • How are their mobility, eyesight and hearing?
  • Does the shelter have a senior-for-senior or hospice foster program?

Budget for care

Plan for regular check-ups, possible dental work and medications. Ask local vets about senior wellness plans. Find a vet near you.

Making home comfortable

A thick bed, rugs on slippery floors, a ramp or steps for the couch or car, and short, regular walks go a long way.

Where to find senior dogs

Search shelters near you and look at each shelter's adoptable dogs page; many let you filter by age. Watch our adoption events calendar for reduced-fee events.
