How to adopt a dog from a shelter

Adopting from a shelter is usually quicker and simpler than people expect. Here's how it typically works.

1. Find shelters near you

Search by city or state. Look at more than one: city and county shelters, humane societies and SPCAs all have different dogs.

2. Browse adoptable dogs online

Every shelter page links to the shelter's own adoptable animals. Read the notes: energy level, how the dog does with kids, cats and other dogs.

3. Visit or apply

Some shelters are walk-in; others want an online application first. Bring a photo ID. Renters may be asked for landlord approval.

4. Meet the dog

Staff will set up a meet-and-greet. If you have a dog at home, many shelters ask you to bring them so the two can meet. Ask about history, health and behavior.

5. Pay the fee and take them home

The fee usually includes spay or neuter, vaccines and a microchip. See adoption fees and watch for fee-waived events.

The first weeks home

Give your dog a quiet space, a steady routine and time. Many trainers talk about the "3-3-3" pattern: about 3 days to decompress, 3 weeks to learn the routine and 3 months to feel at home.

Not sure yet?

Many shelters let you take a dog out for a day or host a sleepover first. Find a dog day out program.

Not ready to adopt? ShelterDayOut, our sister site, lists shelters that let you take a dog out for the day.
