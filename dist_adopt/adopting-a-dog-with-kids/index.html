Adopting a dog with kids

The right shelter dog can be a wonderful family member. A little planning makes the match safer and happier for everyone.

Choosing a dog

  • Ask staff which dogs have lived with or met children, and how it went.
  • Look for a dog who's relaxed with handling, noise and busy rooms.
  • Consider energy level: a calm adult dog can be easier with young kids than a puppy.
  • Ask about foster notes; fosters often know how a dog does at home with kids.

Bring the family

Many shelters ask that everyone in the household meet the dog before adopting. Keep the first meeting calm: let the dog approach, and have kids stand still and offer a hand to sniff.

House rules for kids

  • Leave the dog alone while eating, sleeping or chewing.
  • No hugging, climbing on or chasing the dog.
  • Always ask an adult before playing.
  • Give the dog a safe space, like a crate or bed, that kids stay out of.

Supervise early on

Watch kids and the dog together for the first weeks, especially with young children. Learn the signs a dog needs space: lip licking, yawning, turning away, stiffening or growling.
