Your first week with a rescue dog

⁠‍‌‍‌​‌​⁢‍⁢​⁢‌‍⁢​⁠Most newly adopted dogs need time to decompress. A calm, predictable first week helps them trust you faster.

Before they come home

Set up a quiet spot with a bed or crate, water and a few toys. Block off anything you don't want chewed. Have food ready, ideally what the shelter has been feeding. See the adoption checklist.

Day 1

Go straight home, take them out to the bathroom spot on a leash, then let them explore one or two rooms. Keep visitors away and the house quiet. Some dogs sleep a lot; some don't eat much at first. Both are common.

Days 2 and 3

Start a routine: meals, walks and bathroom breaks at the same times each day. Keep walks short and on leash, away from dog parks and crowds. Reward calm behavior and going potty outside.

Days 4 to 7

If they're settling, add short training sessions (sit, name, come), a little alone time so they learn you come back, and a few new places. Introduce other pets slowly and supervised.

The first vet visit

Book a check-up in the first week or two and bring the shelter's records. Some shelters include a free first visit with a partner vet. Find a vet near you.

When to ask for help

Call the shelter if you're worried about growling, snapping, not eating for more than a day or two, or anything else. Most shelters would rather help early, and many have free behavior help lines for adopters.

The 3-3-3 pattern

Many trainers describe about 3 days to decompress, 3 weeks to learn the routine and 3 months to feel at home. Every dog is different, so go at their pace.
